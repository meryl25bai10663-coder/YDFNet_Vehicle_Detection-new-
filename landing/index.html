EmergeRoute proposes traffic-control policies, runs every one in a full SUMO simulation, and ranks them on six objectives so the trade-offs are visible.

Every candidate is simulated, not estimated.

Six stages run in order each time you generate policies.

  1. Probe the network

    A short live SUMO run measures queue lengths at every signalized intersection.

  2. Predict congestion

    An optional XGBoost model forecasts near-future congestion from logged simulation history.

  3. Generate candidates

    A rule-based generator proposes explainable actions: signal timing, network-wide changes, emergency corridors.

  4. Simulate each one

    Every candidate runs end to end in SUMO and returns measured results.

  5. Score with NSGA-II

    Candidates are ranked across all six objectives at once, so no single metric decides.

  6. Recommend with reasons

    The top policy appears with a plain-language explanation and a comparison against doing nothing.

Six objectives, shown side by side.

Policies that trade one objective for another stay visible instead of being hidden by a single score.

Congestion delay
Average time vehicles lose compared with free flow.

Fairness
The gap between the best and worst waiting times across vehicles.

Decision support, not signal control.

Results come from simulation. Recommendations are never applied to real traffic signals.

The built-in network is a synthetic grid. The real-area tool lets you pick a place and simulate its actual street layout, but the traffic demand is a setting you choose, not something measured at that location.

One policy type, signal timing, is fully tested end to end.
